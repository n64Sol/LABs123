# 1. Core concept

## Game title

**Labyrinths**

## Core hook

**Own a Labyrinth. Build its Chambers. Attract adventurers. Earn when they run it.**

## Short pitch

100 years ago, mysterious Labyrinth entrances began appearing across the world. Every connected wallet can claim one entrance. Behind each entrance is a player-owned Labyrinth filled with magical Chambers, rare nodes, monsters, chests, merchants, bosses, and treasures.

Players explore a bright high-fantasy overworld, discover other players’ Labyrinth entrances, preview them, and choose which ones to run.

Every Labyrinth is free by default.

Owners earn **20% of the drops generated by visitors** who run their Labyrinth, whether the Labyrinth is free or paid.

Owners can also unlock a **Toll Gate**. Once unlocked, they can set any token entry fee they want. When a visitor pays to enter:

* 80% of the entry fee goes to the Labyrinth owner
* 20% of the entry fee goes to the treasury

The better the Labyrinth, the more visitors it attracts, the more drops it generates, and the more valuable it becomes as an owned game space.

# 2. Lore

100 years ago, strange magical entrances began appearing across the world.

At first, people thought they were ruins, portals, or old temple gates. But every entrance led somewhere different. Some opened into crystal gardens. Others led to sunken temples, lava forges, sky shrines, monster markets, ancient libraries, mushroom forests, and treasure vaults.

The entrances became known as **Labyrinths**.

No two Labyrinths are exactly alike. Each one is shaped by its owner. Adventurers travel the overworld looking for the best Labyrinths to run, the rarest nodes to harvest, the strongest bosses to challenge, and the most profitable treasures to discover.

Every wallet can awaken one dormant Labyrinth entrance.

Some owners build free Labyrinths to attract traffic and earn from visitor drops. Others unlock Toll Gates, invest heavily into rare Chambers, and charge entry for premium runs.

The world is now filled with glowing entrances, competing owners, treasure hunters, rare biomes, seasonal events, and player-owned magical businesses.

# 6. Core economy rules

## Rule 1: Every Labyrinth is free by default

New Labyrinths start as free-to-enter.

Players can publish free Labyrinths and earn from visitor drop share.

## Rule 2: Owners earn 20% of visitor drops

Whenever a visitor runs a Labyrinth and earns loot, the Labyrinth owner receives a 20% owner share of the drop value.

This applies to both:

* free Labyrinths
* paid Labyrinths

Example:

Visitor runs a free Labyrinth and earns:

* 100 Gold
* 20 Ore
* 5 Dust

Owner receives bonus owner-share rewards equal to 20% of the drop value:

* 20 Gold
* 4 Ore
* 1 Dust

The visitor still keeps their full displayed reward. The owner share should be treated as additional generated owner earnings or a clearly tracked owner-share reward, not as something stolen from the visitor.

## Rule 3: Owners can unlock Toll Gate

Owners can pay premium currency/token to unlock a Toll Gate.

Once Toll Gate is unlocked, the owner can set any entry fee they want.

Entry fee can be:

* zero
* low
* medium
* high
* extremely high if the owner wants

The market decides whether visitors are willing to pay.

## Rule 4: Paid entry fees split 80/20

When a visitor enters a paid Labyrinth:

* visitor pays the full entry fee
* owner receives 80%
* treasury receives 20%

Example:

Entry fee: 100 $LAB

* Visitor pays: 100 $LAB
* Owner receives: 80 $LAB
* Treasury receives: 20 $LAB

## Rule 5: Paid Labyrinths still generate drop-share earnings

Paid entry is extra revenue.

Owners of paid Labyrinths still also receive 20% of the visitor’s drop value.

So a paid run can generate:

* entry fee revenue
* drop-share revenue
* ratings
* traffic
* prestige

## Rule 6: Owners buy infrastructure, not guaranteed profit

Owners can upgrade rare nodes, boss rooms, treasure chambers, merchants, reward capacity, and premium biomes.

But owners should not directly buy uncapped profit.

The game should imply that better Labyrinths attract more visitors and better ratings, but visitors decide what is worth running.

# 7. Core game loop

The core loop is:

1. Player connects mock wallet.
2. Player claims a Labyrinth entrance.
3. Player enters their own Labyrinth.
4. Player collects loot from nodes, chests, monsters, and Chamber interactions.
5. Player earns soft currencies.
6. Player upgrades Chambers, rare nodes, chests, monsters, bosses, merchants, and biomes.
7. Player publishes the Labyrinth to the overworld.
8. Other players discover it.
9. Other players run it.
10. Owner earns 20% of visitor drop value.
11. Owner unlocks Toll Gate.
12. Owner sets any entry fee.
13. Visitors decide whether the Labyrinth is worth paying for.
14. Paid visitor enters.
15. Entry fee is split 80% owner / 20% treasury.
16. Paid run still generates owner drop-share earnings.
17. Visitor rates the Labyrinth.
18. Better Labyrinths rank higher in discovery.
19. Owner reinvests into better Chambers, rare nodes, bosses, and reward quality.

**Drop Share**

Owners earn 20% of visitor drop value from every run, free or paid.

**Toll Gate locked**

Unlock Toll Gate to set an entry fee and earn from paid visitors.

**Toll Gate active**

Visitors must pay your selected entry fee to enter this Labyrinth.

**Owner entry share**

You receive 80% of every paid entry. The treasury receives 20%.

**Rare nodes**

Rare nodes increase the appeal of your Labyrinth and improve possible rewards.

**Boss active**

Boss rooms attract adventurers seeking higher-risk, higher-reward runs.

**Entry fee warning**

High entry fees may reduce traffic unless your Labyrinth has strong ratings, rare nodes, or boss rewards.

# 25. MVP goal

The goal is not to build the full final game yet.

The goal is to prove the core loop:

1. Player connects mock wallet.
2. Player claims a Labyrinth.
3. Player upgrades it.
4. Player publishes it.
5. Other players discover it in the overworld.
6. Visitors can enter free or paid Labyrinths.
7. Paid entry splits 80% to owner and 20% to treasury.
8. Owners earn 20% of visitor drops from both free and paid runs.
9. Player can run a simple Chamber and collect mock loot.
10. Run summary shows visitor rewards and owner earnings.
11. The game structure is ready for future VTT-style room imports.

---

# 26. Important constraints

* Do not make a generic dungeon crawler.
* Do not make a random tilemap generator.
* Do not make the whole game dark fantasy.
* Use high fantasy as the main visual direction.
* Do not focus on final monster or final character art yet.
* Use placeholders where needed.
* Prioritize the ownership loop, overworld discovery, Toll Gate economy, drop-share earnings, Labyrinth upgrade system, and room/chamber architecture.
* The final product should feel like a polished player-owned Labyrinth economy game, not just a combat demo.

Final reminder:

**The owner earns from two sources:**

1. **20% of visitor drop value from every run, free or paid**
2. **80% of paid entry fees when Toll Gate is active**

The treasury earns:

1. **20% of paid entry fees**

Build the prototype around these rules.
